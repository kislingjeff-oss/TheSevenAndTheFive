The Seven and the Five

Down the side are seven systems whose failures multiply. Across the top are five principles of Revolutionary Intercommunal Mutualism for building what replaces them. Each cell turns one system and one principle into a question a community, or a Quaker meeting, can ask itself. Choose any cell, row or column.

Jeff Kisling

Arrow keys move between cells once one is selected.

What the grid shows

  1. Each list covers what the other leaves out. The UPP's infrastructure stack is strongest on food, energy and community safety. It has no pillar for War or Spirit and treats Money only in passing. Those three rows are where Quaker practice has the most to offer.
  2. Spirit holds the grid together. Every principle depends on people doing slow, unpaid work over years, and Spirit supplies that motive. Channels sit in the Spirit row and hold up every other row too, which is why Channels Between Nodes calls them the layer beneath the stack, not a sixth pillar.
  3. Both vocabularies stay intact. The principles keep the UPP's own names and the systems keep yours. Nothing here renames one in the other's terms.

Open questions

  • Which list of principles. These five come from the UPP's post of 16 February 2026. Their reader's guide lists nine, including Survival Pending Revolution, Subsidiarity and Anticipatory Resilience. The nine are more complete; the five fit a grid more easily.
  • Whether order matters. The UPP's Lesson Three says its stack is built in sequence. The Channels work has treated it as built in no particular order. The two readings need to be reconciled, or the difference stated.
  • Health and technology. The UPP names both as pillars; the seven systems do not. Do they sit inside the existing seven, or run across all of them?
